1. THE DESIGN DECISION I HAVE NOT MADE — DECIDE IT AND TELL ME
================================================================================
This is the crux of the phase and I deliberately have not chosen for you.

Sales' routes run on the SHARED `apiHandler` / `resolveWorkspace` from
`packages/platform-api`, behind an `AppContext`. `resolveWorkspace` reads
`platform.workspaces` and `platform.workspace_members`. **After this phase,
sales' workspaces live in `sales.workspaces`.** So one of these has to happen:

  (a) `AppContext` gains a workspace resolver — each app supplies where its
      workspaces live, and the shared handler keeps doing auth, the error log,
      the version headers and the error envelope.

  (b) Sales stops using the shared `resolveWorkspace` and writes its own,
      keeping `apiHandler` for everything else.

  (c) Something better you see and I do not.

**My lean is (a)**, because it keeps the split honest: the plumbing is genuinely
shared, the DATA is not, and that distinction is the whole refactor in one line.
It also means app #3 inherits a resolver-shaped hole rather than copying a
resolver. But (a) touches a file both apps depend on, and if the interface comes
out ugly that is a real argument for (b).

**Decide, implement, and defend it in your reply.** If you pick (a), issues must
be provably unaffected — its resolver keeps reading `platform.*` and its tests
prove it.

2. THE THING MY PLAN MISSED ENTIRELY — 12 FOREIGN KEYS
================================================================================
I checked production while writing this brief. **Twelve of sales' tables have a
foreign key on `platform.workspaces`:**

    prospects · contacts · stage_entries · meetings · communications ·
    and seven more (query pg_constraint, do not trust this list)

Once sales' workspaces live in `sales.workspaces`, those FKs point at the wrong
table. This is not optional and it is not Phase 3's — a prospect that references
an issues workspace is the coupling this refactor removes.

**How to do it without deleting anything:**

Sales' domain data in production is one prospect and one communication, both in
workspace id 1. So MIRROR rather than wipe:

  1. Copy the `platform.workspaces` rows that sales actually uses into
     `sales.workspaces`, **preserving the id**, and the matching
     `platform.workspace_members` rows into `sales.workspace_members`
  2. Advance `sales.workspaces`' sequence past the highest copied id
  3. Drop each FK and re-add it against `sales.workspaces`

Preserving ids makes step 3 a constraint swap with no data movement, and means
**this phase still deletes nothing** — Phase 3 remains the only phase that does,
which is the property that makes "where did the row go" answerable.

The two tables will then drift, and that is correct: after this, a person's
issues workspace and their sales workspace are different things that happen to
share an id today.

`sales.prospect_labels → platform.labels` is the thirteenth FK. **Leave it.**
Labels are Phase 3's.

3. WHAT TO BUILD
================================================================================

--- 3a. Self-signup, WITH THE WHITELIST. The security-critical part. ----------
Sales has no register route at all. Copy issues'
(`apps/issues/app/api/auth/register/route.ts`) and point it at sales' tables.

**It MUST carry the whitelist gate** — `SUPER_ADMINS` + `platform.email_whitelist`
— PLAN.md §6 decision 1. This is the one thing in the phase where a mistake is
not a bug but an open door: the account it creates is the SHARED platform
account, so an ungated register route on sales is an ungated register route on
the whole platform, issues included.

Watch that gate fail. A non-whitelisted email must be refused, and you must see
it refused, not reason that it would be.

--- 3b. A workspace on first sign-in -------------------------------------------
`apps/sales/lib/auth.ts` currently does NOT create a workspace, deliberately
(D-3): with no switcher and no create flow, a workspace minted at sign-in was one
the human could neither see nor leave. **That reasoning was right and its
premise is now gone** — sales owns its workspaces and shows a members page.

On a first sign-in (both Google and credentials), in ONE transaction:
create `sales.workspaces` + the owner's `sales.workspace_members` row.

One transaction matters: a user with a workspace and no membership is locked out
of their own data, and it is the shape that survives a partial failure.

Read the header of `apps/sales/lib/auth.ts` before you change it — it explains
why it does nothing today, and your commit should explain why that changed.

--- 3c. The members page, visible by default -----------------------------------
`apps/sales/app/dashboard/settings/members/` — list members, invite by email,
change role, remove. **This is the screen the whole refactor is for.** Sales'
settings today has account/preferences/profile/tokens and no members page.

Sales' own visual language, not a copy of issues' (D-4). `apps/sales/lib/pipeline.ts`
is where its colours are decided — agent 2 got caught by a guard for importing
issues' grey.

--- 3d. Serve sales' own workspace routes --------------------------------------
`app/api/workspaces/**`, `members`, `invitations`, `invite-candidates`,
`/api/me/pending-invitations` — stop mounting the platform factories, serve
sales' tables.

--- 3e. Delete the gate --------------------------------------------------------
Remove the "No access to b/sales" screen and the `requireAppAccess` /
`PLATFORM_ENFORCE_APP_ACCESS` check from sales. **A member of a sales workspace
is a sales user.** The env var stays set in Vercel until Phase 5 removes it;
your job is that sales stops consulting it.

--- 3f. What stays hidden ------------------------------------------------------
No workspace switcher, no create-workspace flow, no workspace settings page. One
workspace per user, invisible. The tables support more; the UI does not offer it.
That is PLAN.md §1's sales-specific rule, and it is the user's stated preference,
not an implementation shortcut.
